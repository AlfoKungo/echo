Opening Echo…

A little practice, your way.

Eight rooms. Home has a tile for each, with one live hint on it. Vocabulary is your word cards. Practise mixes everything together. Listen plays French and asks what you understood; Read shows French and asks the same. Walkman is a tape you just listen to. Sounds trains your ear. Story is a serial written for you, and Radio is a short episode each day. Underneath, Grammar and Mistakes are there when you want one thing at a time, and Statistics shows how your words are doing: how many you know, how their scores spread from shaky to solid (tap a bar to see its words), your hardest words, each category, your answers over the last two weeks, and your Listen and Read levels. Nothing there is a target.

Words from a song (in Vocabulary options): search for a French song, pick the right version (covers that sing the same words are grouped together), and Claude (Opus) reads it in the background, usually in under a minute — each word in its dictionary form: the infinitive for a verb, the masculine singular for an adjective, a noun with le, la or l’ and its gender. Names and sounds are left out, and words you already have are marked. You untick anything you don’t want, and the rest become a deck named after the song. Each word joins your practice once it has been recorded, before your other new words or mixed in with them, as you choose in the options. The song’s page says how much of what is sung you already know, and Read the song shows it line by line: › at a line’s start gives its meaning, and a tapped word its note. The lyrics stay on your Mac.

Story is a serial in French, one episode at a time, written for you by Claude (Opus) from what you know: nearly every word is one you practise or have understood in Listen and Read, with a handful of new words and one grammar point a step ahead. Tap a word for its meaning (a new one says so), › for a line’s, listen to the episode read aloud, and answer three questions. When you finish, the next episode is written: a step harder if you looked up little and answered everything right, a step easier if you looked up a lot or missed questions, and the words you looked up come back in it. Writing takes a few minutes.

Radio is one episode a day, a few minutes long, made on your Mac: today’s words in sentences, each followed by its meaning; the latest story episode, line by line with its English; and one grammar point, explained in English with French examples. Like the Walkman it keeps playing with your phone locked, and the lock-screen buttons skip a part at a time. A new episode is ready each morning after 04:00.

Sounds trains your ear on pairs of phrases that differ by one sound — le chat and les chats, tu and tout, bon and banc. Echo plays one; you choose which you heard, then can hear both. The pairs you confuse come back first. Every recording was checked to be heard as itself and not its twin; a pair whose recording failed that check is left out.

Colours and hooks. Nouns are coloured by gender — blue for masculine, pink for feminine — wherever Echo shows French (switch it off in Vocabulary options). A word you keep missing gets a short memory hook on its card, written by Claude, to help it stick.

Walkman plays a French word, pauses so you can find its meaning, says the English, then the French again — word after word, and it keeps playing with your phone locked. Every fifth word is new, heard three times close together. The rest are words you have started, picked one of two ways, chosen under the player; a third choice plays sentences instead of words. By score (the default): every word has a score that rises when you recall it (+2) or hear it to the end on the Walkman (+0.5), drops when you miss it (−3), and fades by half every three weeks; words with low scores are far likelier to be picked, but any word can come up. By groups: words you are learning first (the ones you miss most at the front), then those due for review, then known words about to fade. Either way a word waits at least 15 others before it returns, and listening never changes your vocabulary schedule. Sentences plays the Listen and Read sentences up to your Read level the same way — French, a pause to understand it, the English, the French again — each once per tape, with sentences holding your due and shakiest words likelier to come first; hearing them changes no scores. What you heard lists the last 50 finished words and sentences, newest first: the speaker plays one again (the tape pauses and carries on), ✓ and ✗ let you say afterwards whether you knew it — counted like a recall (+2) or a miss (−3) in its score, tap again to take it back — and a word you haven’t started can go straight into your Vocabulary practice. Marks move only the Walkman score, never your vocabulary schedule.

Listen and Read never end. Each question is chosen for you from graded French — single sentences, short dialogues and short texts, A1 towards B1. You choose the meaning, type a word you heard, write down the whole sentence, or answer a question about a passage. The small A1 · A2 · B1 label shows the level Echo is choosing from: it drifts up while you keep answering well and eases back when you don’t. Nothing is scored or counted.

In Listen the recording plays by itself when the question appears, and a passage plays line by line with the line number while it runs. The speaker button replays it, and the French stays hidden until you answer. If your browser blocks playback, a large play button appears — tap it once and Echo carries on by itself.

Tap a word. Every French word Echo has a meaning for is underlined: tap it to see what it means in that sentence, and Add to my words puts it into your vocabulary practice (it tells you if the word is already there, or makes it due again). In Read, looking a word up before you answer counts as help, so a right answer is recorded as “with help”. After you answer, tap as freely as you like.

After each answer you see whether you were right, the expected answer — for a dictation, the words you missed — a short explanation, and the whole French with its English, line by line. One button continues; Enter does the same.

Practise is one endless stream that alternates understanding, vocabulary and grammar: a comprehension question, a vocabulary card, another comprehension question, a grammar question, skipping anything with nothing waiting. The small label says which you are in. Grammar here is a quiet round across the topics you have already touched, and it never shows a score.

Mistakes gathers what keeps catching you out: a grammar topic you miss, words you keep missing, sentences you didn’t catch. Tap a group to practise just that — a grammar group alternates its grammar questions with the sentences that use it.

Vocabulary. Recall the word, then tap to reveal its translation. Tap the left half if you didn’t know it, or the right half if you did. Space or Enter reveals; the arrow keys grade. Undo takes back your latest answer.

Two successful recalls after your latest miss finish a practice cycle; a miss resets the count to zero and moves the card to the failed stack. FSRS schedules the next review. Each direction has its own memory. Category, daily allowance and pronunciation speed live in Vocabulary options.

Your working deck has three stacks: unseen, last answered incorrectly, and last answered correctly. Unseen counts the first stack plus new words available within your daily allowance. Seen counts the other two stacks together. Finished cards count in neither. Undo restores the card, its stack and the counts.

The first ten unseen cards come first. After that, practice mixes three unseen, two failed and one successful card, skipping empty stacks. Every card enters or re-enters a stack at a random position. The daily allowance renews at 04:00 on your Mac; unfinished cards keep their stacks and progress.

Extra practice. In Vocabulary options, or when you reach the end, add +5, +10, +20 or any number of new words for today only; your daily allowance is unchanged and the extra ends at 04:00. You can also review finished words early — 10, 20 or any number of the ones due soonest, or the ones you missed most. Asking for more than are available takes all of them. An early review is a real review: finishing it updates the word’s schedule, with a smaller step because it came early.

Your words (the search button in Vocabulary) lists every word. Choose Studied to see the words you have practised, most recent first, with when you last studied each, how often you missed it and its next review. Hide translations lets you test yourself and reveal each answer; checking the list never changes your progress.

Grammar & sentences. Seven units take you from A1 towards B1. Open a topic to read its rule, then practise a round of 10 questions: build, rewrite, complete, repair, choose, translate and conjugate. Check your answer, try again with specific feedback, or ask for a hint or the rule; each round records what you did on your own, with help, or need to revisit. Ctrl/⌘ + Enter checks or continues. Once a question is answered, a speaker button reads the correct French aloud where a recording exists.

Tests. A topic test asks 15 questions across the whole topic, and each unit ends with a test of 20 questions across its topics. Tests have no hints, rule or shown answers, and each question takes one try; you see the right answer and why straight after. Your best and latest scores are kept, and anything you miss in a test comes back first the next time you practise that topic.

Audio is your choice in Vocabulary. It starts off. Turn Audio on to hear the French recording each time you explicitly reveal a card, in either direction. Turn on Listen (French → English) to hear each word instead of reading it. Listen and Read always play their recordings; the pronunciation speed in Vocabulary options — normal or slower — applies everywhere.

Your vocabulary progress, grammar rounds, comprehension levels and mistakes live on your Mac, shared by your phone and desktop. Your direction, category and audio choices are remembered on this device. Echo always opens on Home, and Back returns to the previous screen.
